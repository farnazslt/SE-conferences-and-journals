# Software Engineering Journals

The main journals for software engineering research. Unlike conferences, journals accept submissions **continuously (rolling)** — there is no annual deadline, except for special issues, which *do* have fixed deadlines and are often a faster, well-scoped route.

Impact factors below are **approximate, from the most recent Journal Citation Reports / publisher pages at the time of writing (Oct 2026)**. They change every year — verify the current figure on the publisher's journal page before quoting it.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSE | IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering | IEEE | ≈ 7.4 | Rolling |
| TOSEM | ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology | ACM | ≈ 4.4 | Rolling |
| EMSE | Empirical Software Engineering | Springer | ≈ 3.8 | Rolling |
| IST | Information and Software Technology | Elsevier | ≈ 3.8 | Rolling |

| JSEP | Journal of Software: Evolution and Process | Wiley | ≈ 2 | Rolling |

## Choosing between them

- **TSE and TOSEM** are the flagship, most selective SE journals — best for mature, archival work.
- **EMSE** is the natural home for strong empirical studies.
- **JSS and IST** are broad, high-volume, and well-regarded for solid applied and empirical work.
- **Topic journals** (REJ for requirements, SoSyM for modelling, ASE journal for automation, STVR for testing) can be a better fit than a generalist venue when the contribution is topic-specific.
- **IEEE Software** is a practitioner-facing magazine, not a research journal — great for experience reports and transferable lessons, written in a different style.

{: .note }
> **Journal-first option:** TSE, TOSEM and EMSE papers are frequently invited for journal-first presentation at ICSE / FSE, so a journal submission can still get you a conference talk.
